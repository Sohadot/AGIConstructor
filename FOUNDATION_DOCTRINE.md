# Foundation Doctrine — AGI Construction

**The classificatory position from which AGIConstructor and ACL are derived.**

Status: Foundation document

> Whatever form general intelligence takes, it will have been constructed. Every component is auditable.

---

## 1. The position

AGIConstructor takes a **classificatory position**, not a predictive one.

The predictive question — *has general intelligence arrived, and when will it?* — cannot be settled. It depends on a definition of "general intelligence" that no two parties share, and it resolves into argument rather than evidence. Predictions age badly, invite advocacy, and cannot be audited.

The classificatory position replaces that question with one that survives regardless of when — or whether — anyone agrees that AGI has arrived:

> A general-capability system is **assembled**. It is built from components. Each component either exists or does not, is evidenced or is not, and can be examined component by component.

This holds whatever form general intelligence eventually takes. A system need not *be* AGI for its construction to be auditable. The doctrine does not wait on a threshold; it records what was built.

**This is the whole move.** We do not adjudicate arrival. We classify construction.

## 2. Why "construction" is the operative frame

"Construction" is chosen deliberately over "capability," "intelligence," or "generality."

- **Capability** invites measurement of degree, which invites ranking, which invites advocacy. Construction is binary at the component level — a thing is built or it is not — and defers degree entirely.
- **Intelligence** is contested and undefined. Construction is observable: it leaves artifacts, disclosures, and reproducible behavior.
- **Generality** is the subject of the dispute we are declining to enter. Construction is neutral to it.

A constructed component is a claim with a surface that can be examined. That surface — and only that surface — is what this asset governs.

## 3. The canonical relationship between AGIConstructor and ACL

These are **not the same thing**, and conflating them weakens both.

| Layer | What it is | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **AGIConstructor** | The reference layer for AGI Construction as a category | Holds the doctrine, the ontology of audit surfaces, the audit instruments, the ledgers, and (in future) the registry |
| **ACL** | One instrument within that layer | Classifies, per component, what a specific system demonstrably possesses and at what evidentiary standard |

ACL is **an instrument of proof**, not the definition of the category. AGIConstructor is the reference; ACL is one tool the reference publishes. Other instruments may be added over time without changing the doctrine.

The layer stack is set out in [ARCHITECTURE.md](./ARCHITECTURE.md):

> Foundation Doctrine → Construction Ontology → ACL audit instrument → Ledgers → Validator / Registry (future)

Reading ACL as "all of AGIConstructor" is the error this document exists to prevent. ACL is the fourth-layer evidence ledger. It presupposes the first two layers; it does not replace them.

## 4. The three distinctions that must never collapse

An auditable record depends on keeping three things separate. Collapsing any pair reintroduces the argument we are declining to have.

1. **What exists in a system.** The actual construction — what was in fact built, whether or not anyone outside can see it. A closed system may possess a component that no external party can confirm.
2. **What is publicly evidenced.** The subset of construction that is disclosed, demonstrated, or independently reproduced — and at what tier of evidence.
3. **What the instrument can currently classify.** The subset of the evidenced world that ACL-1.0's states, tiers, and disqualifiers are able to resolve.

A ledger reports the **intersection** of these three. It is not a claim about (1) beyond what (2) supports, and it is bounded by (3). Where (1) exceeds (2) — a component is built but not evidenced — the honest finding is *not demonstrated*, recorded with an explicit access limit, never *absent*. Absence is a finding, and a finding requires evidence.

## 5. Independence from safety and capability

The doctrine governs one axis only: **construction**. Two adjacent axes are deliberately excluded.

- **Safety / alignment / risk.** Independent axis. A fully constructed system may be unsafe; a safe system may be sparsely constructed. Construction findings must never be read as safety assurances.
- **Capability / performance / quality.** Also independent. "Constructed" means present, repeatable, and evidenced — not good, not powerful, not close to any threshold. More constructed components do not mean a more capable system.

These exclusions are not modesty. They are what keeps the record auditable. The moment a construction ledger is read as a safety verdict or a capability score, it stops being a record and becomes an opinion.

## 6. What follows from this doctrine

- Instruments derived from this doctrine (ACL and any successors) **classify; they do not conclude.** A complete ledger is a record of what was found, never a verdict that a system is or is not AGI.
- The set of audit surfaces is **descriptive of current constructed systems**, not a definition of intelligence and not a set of necessary or sufficient conditions. It is expected to change. See [CONSTRUCTION_ONTOLOGY.md](./CONSTRUCTION_ONTOLOGY.md).
- Published instruments are **frozen and versioned.** ACL-1.0 will not be silently modified; limitations identified after publication are recorded as candidates for a future version (see [ACL-2.0-CANDIDATES.md](./ACL-2.0-CANDIDATES.md)), not retrofitted into the published text.
